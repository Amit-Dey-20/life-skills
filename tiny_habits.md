## Question 2
### How can you use B = MAP to make making new habits easier? What are M, A and P
### Answer:

B = MAP is a behavior model that explains that a we can change our behavior only by three things come together:

- M = Motivation: How much we want to do the behavior.
- A = Ability: How easy or difficult the behavior is to do.
- P = Prompt: A reminder that tells us to do the behavior.

To make a new habit easier make the behavior very small and easy because if work is easy and small its not need to much motivation and connect it to something we already do that helps to reminder the work than after work is complete celebrate for it.

## Question 3
### Why it is important to "Shine" or Celebrate after each successful completion of habit? (This is the most important concept in today's topic. Whatever you celebrate becomes a habit)
### Answer:
It is important to celebrate after completing a habit because it creates a positive feeling. This positive feeling helps our brain remember the behavior and makes us want to do it again.

For example, if I want to build a habit of reading, I can read one page and say “Awesome!” after doing it. This small celebration makes me feel good about completing the habit. When we repeat this process the behavior becomes easier and more automatic.

## Question 5
### What is the book's perspective about Identity?
### Answer:
The book says that identity is the most important part of changing our habits. Instead of only focusing on what we want to achieve, we should focus on the person we want to become.

For example, instead of saying “I want to read more books,” we can think “I am a reader.” Instead of saying “I want to exercise,” we can think “I am a fit and healthy person.”

Our daily habits help create our identity. Every time we do something, we are giving evidence about the type of person we want to become.

## Question 6
### Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit easier to do?
### Answer:
The book says that to make a habit easier we should remove unnecessary effort and make the habit simple to start. If a habit is difficult or takes too much effort we are more likely to avoid it.

## Question 7
### Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit harder to do?
### Answer:
The book says that if we want to stop a bad habit, we should make it harder and less convenient to do. When we add some extra effort between ourselves and the bad habit we are less likely to do it.

## Question 8:
### Pick one habit that you would like to do more of? What are the steps that you can take to make it make the cue obvious or the habit more attractive or easy and or response satisfying?
### Answer:
The habit I want to add is daily exercise.

I can make this habit easier by taking small steps:-

- Make the cue obvious: Keep my shoes and exercise clothes where I can see them and it will give me reminder.
- Make it attractive: Listen to my favourite music while exercising.
- Make it easy: Initially I start with 10–15 minutes of exercise every day then I gradually increase it.
- Make it satisfying: Mark a tick on my calendar after completing the exercise.

If I follow these steps every day, exercise will slowly become a regular habit.

## Question 9:
### Pick one habit that you would like to eliminate or do less of? What are the steps that you can take to make it make the cue invisible or the process unattractive or hard or the response unsatisfying?
### Answer:
The bad habit I want to eliminate is skipping breakfast. I want to make eating breakfast a regular habit because it is important to start my day properly.

I can take these steps:

- Make the cue invisible: Keep unhealthy snacks away from my room so I don't replace breakfast with snacks.
- Make the process unattractive: Remind myself that skipping breakfast can make me feel tired and less focused during the morning.
- Make the habit harder: Avoid staying in bed until the last minute. Wake up early enough to have time for breakfast.
- Make skipping unsatisfying:- Keep a daily checklist and mark whether I ate breakfast. This will motivate me to avoid skipping it.
